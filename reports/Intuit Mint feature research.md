# Mint won on effortless budgeting, then vanished

Mint (Mint.com, later Intuit Mint) was a free web and mobile personal-finance app. Aaron Patzer founded it in 2006, launched it at TechCrunch40 in September 2007, and sold it to Intuit in 2009 for about $170M. Intuit shut it down on March 23, 2024 and steered users to Credit Karma. Mint's appeal came down to three things that took no effort from the user. It pulled every bank, card, loan and investment account into one screen. It categorized transactions automatically. And it compared that spending against simple monthly category budgets shown as green, yellow and red bars. Around that core sat Trends charts, net worth, goals, bill reminders, alerts and a free credit score, all paid for by referral offers ("Ways to Save") that users came to resent. When Mint closed, Credit Karma kept accounts, transactions and net worth but dropped category budgets, custom categories, bill reminders and goals. Those were the features users protested losing, which shows where Mint's real value lay. Paul's spec already covers the core: aggregation through his Plaid server, auto-categorization, recategorizing, monthly category budgets, net-worth history and holdings. The biggest gaps are transaction rules, splits, tags, rollover budgets, goals, bills and alerts, and most of them need server changes that Paul must approve. **Source caveat:** the researchers could not fetch full pages, because a network proxy blocked nearly every domain. Almost every fact below comes from search-engine snippets of the cited URLs, so exact wording and some figures need checking against the original pages. Uncertain and conflicting claims are flagged where they appear.

## A $31M startup became a $170M Intuit side project

Patzer built Mint because tracking his own money in Quicken and Microsoft Money took hours he didn't have while working about 75-hour weeks ([Topgrading](https://blog.topgrading.com/case-studies/mint); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/videos/the-minting-of-mintcom/)). He raised a **$4.7M Series A in April 2007** led by Shasta Ventures ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/17608/mint-raises-47-million-in-series-a-funding)), a **$12M Series B in March 2008** led by Benchmark ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2008/03/05/mint-gets-a-mint/)) and a **$14M Series C in August 2009** led by DAG Ventures ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2009/08/12/full-details-on-mints-14-million-series-c-round)). Mint launched publicly at TechCrunch40 in September 2007 and won the **$50,000 top prize**. It offered automatic transaction import, "patent-pending" categorization, and alerts for bills, low balances and unusual spending ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2007/09/18/mint-wins-techcrunch40-50000-award/)). Mint served both the US and Canada ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/mint/)), which matters for Paul. Some secondary sources date Mint from its 2007 launch rather than its 2006 founding.

Growth was fast. Mint had about 160,000 users six months after launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2008/03/05/mint-gets-a-mint/)) and **1.5M users** when Intuit agreed to buy it ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/intuit-to-acquire-mintcom-for-170-million)). Intuit's FY2010 earnings release put it at more than 3M ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012310079160/f56699exv99w01.htm)). The **iPhone app reached the App Store in December 2008** and quickly topped the finance charts ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/chase_bank_mint_top_the_charts_with_new_iphone_apps/)), and Android followed in May 2010 ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/956/correcting-and-replacing-android-users-can-manage-money-on-the-go-with-new-mint-com-application)). Intuit signed the deal on September 11, 2009, valued at **about $170M in cash including assumed options**, and closed it on November 2, 2009 ([Intuit 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012309043307/f51041e10vk.htm)). Through 2010 Intuit moved Mint off Yodlee's aggregation onto its own. During the switch, users reported accounts that stopped updating and missing transactions ([CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2010/12/02/pf/mint_leaves_yodlee/index.htm)). That was the first episode in a reputation for flaky connections that Mint never shook.

Later user counts need care. Snippets claim more than 10M users by mid-2012 and more than 20M by about 2016. Neither figure was tied to a primary document, and both almost certainly count registrations ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Mint)). The figure most often cited at the shutdown was **3.6M monthly active users in 2021**, from Intuit data reported by Bloomberg ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/mergers-and-acquisitions/intuit-winds-down-personal-finance-app-mint-it-acquired-in-2009)). Intuit's own January 2021 press release, however, claimed "more than 10 million active annual users" ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/315/mint-reinvents-app-experience-paving-the-way-to-a-better-money-management-solution)). The two numbers measure different things (monthly versus annual activity), but together they show active use far below the headline registration counts.

Intuit grew the product by acquisition and bolted on new features. It bought the bill-pay app Check (formerly Pageonce) for about $360M in 2014 and rebranded it **Mint Bills** ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/618/intuit-to-acquire-check)). Bill pay was folded into Mint.com in March 2017 and **shut down on June 30, 2018 because few people used it** ([Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/payments/mint-shutters-bill-payment-feature/)). A free credit score arrived in late 2014 using Equifax data. On October 2, 2017 it switched to **TransUnion VantageScore 3.0, updated monthly** ([Doctor of Credit](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/mint-com-review-free-credit-score-report-monitoring/)). One researcher's background knowledge put the Equifax launch around 2013; treat 2014 as the sourced date. The January 2021 "All New Mint" redesign added subscription tracking, "Mintsights" insight cards and month-over-month net-worth views ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/196/all-new-mint-ios-app-launched-to-empower-financial-freedom)). Fast Company framed the redesign as an attempt to get Mint "back on track after years of neglect" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90591464/intuit-mint-new-version-and-alternatives)). Late in Mint's life, an iOS-only **Mint Premium at $4.99/month** added ad removal and bill negotiation through Billshark ([Retirely](https://retire.ly/2023/06/29/is-mints-premium-subscription-worth-it/)). Its launch date is unconfirmed.

## Twelve feature areas defined the Mint experience

**Aggregation and accounts.** Mint linked bank, credit card, loan, investment and retirement accounts ([CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/mint-budgeting-app-is-going-away-here-are-some-alternatives/)). From 2010 it valued homes using Zillow's Zestimate ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2010/09/23/mintcom-partners-with-zillow/)), and users could add manual assets such as vehicles, valuables and cash. Kelley Blue Book vehicle values, a 90-day initial history import and daily background refresh are commonly remembered but were **not confirmed** from Mint sources.

**Transactions.** Categories formed a fixed two-level tree, for example Food & Dining › Groceries, Coffee Shops, Restaurants; Auto & Transport › Gas & Fuel, Parking; Bills & Utilities › Mobile Phone, Internet. There were about 22 parent categories ([gist of the 2012 category list](https://gist.github.com/trestletech/4384914)). Users could not delete the defaults, but they could add custom subcategories under any parent. Wanting to hide the defaults was common enough that someone built a third-party browser add-on for it ([schrauger add-on](https://github.com/schrauger/mint.com-customize-default-categories)). A **rule** ("Always rename [payee] and categorize as [category]") applied to **past and future** transactions and was managed from the bottom of the Transactions page ([Mint help](https://help.mint.com/Accounts-and-Transactions/888961281/How-do-I-create-view-and-delete-transaction-rules.htm)). **Splits** divided one purchase across several categories, and the mobile app gained them in May 2012 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/05/23/mint-com-app-update-now-split-transactions)). Built-in **tags** "Reimbursable" and "Tax Related" grouped transactions across categories ([Smart Sister Finance](https://smartsisterfinance.com/resources/how-to-get-the-most-out-of-mintcom)). "Vacation" and "Business" as default tags are unconfirmed. The special category **"Hide from Budgets & Trends"** kept transfers, and refunded purchases paired with their refunds, out of both budgets and reports ([Ford Financial Solutions](https://www.fordfinancialsolutions.com/blog/2017/02/07/best-practices-for-using-mint)). The researchers' background knowledge adds several details, none confirmed by sources: notes, "Mark as duplicate", manual cash transactions, greyed-out pending rows that couldn't be edited, and CSV export of the filtered view.

**Budgets.** Budgets were set per category and were monthly by default. Users could also budget for quarterly or yearly expenses ([juvoni.com](https://juvoni.com/using-mint-com-for-monthly-and-annual-budgeting/)). An optional per-budget **rollover** ("Start each new month with the previous month's leftover amount") came with a "Reset rollover to $0" option ([Annielytics](https://www.annielytics.com/blog/personal/8-ways-customize-mint-make-budgeting-easier/)). The bars were **green when on track, yellow when nearing the limit, red when over** ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/407663/mint-review-financial-budgeting-app.html)). One tutorial ties yellow to pace, for example more than half the budget spent at the halfway point of the month ([Medium, A. Chang](https://amchang.medium.com/the-4-step-quickstart-to-budgeting-on-mint-dbc272c9d02)). That differs from a fixed percentage threshold, and the exact rule is **unconfirmed**. Users widely remember a vertical "today" pacing marker on the bars, but **no Mint source supports it**. One search even returned unrelated hobby-app pull requests that a search summary had mistakenly described as Mint. The "Everything Else" catch-all budget, income budgets and auto-suggested starting budgets also come only from background knowledge.

**Trends, net worth and investments.** Trends offered Spending by Category, Spending by Merchant, Spending Over Time, Net Income Over Time, and Net Worth Over Time with assets and liabilities shown separately. Spending reports could be filtered by time, category, merchant and account ([The Golden Albatross](https://grumpusmaximus.com/track-money-part-4-mint-breakdown/)). Hovering a pie slice showed the total and the number of transactions, and clicking it drilled into the transactions behind it ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/tag/7-advanced-tips-and-tricks-to-make-you-a-mint-expert/)). The Investments tab tracked 401(k)s, IRAs and brokerage accounts, compared them with market benchmarks and flagged fees. Reviewers called it "very weak for all but the basic investors" ([SmartAsset](https://smartasset.com/financial-advisor/mint-review)).

**Goals, bills, alerts and credit.** Goals came in **eight presets**: get out of debt, emergency fund, retirement, home, car, college, trip and home improvement. Each was tied to an account, and Mint calculated the monthly contribution needed to hit the target date ([Money Crashers](https://www.moneycrashers.com/mint-review/)). Alerts covered unusual or large spending, low balances, bills due and going over budget, plus weekly summaries ([Mint alerts page](https://mint.intuit.com/how-mint-works/alerts)). From 2021 they also flagged subscription price changes and bill increases ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/196/all-new-mint-ios-app-launched-to-empower-financial-freedom)). The Apple Watch app showed "how much you can spend today" against a discretionary budget ([Wise Bread](https://www.wisebread.com/8-apple-watch-apps-thatll-save-you-money)). The app could be locked with a 4-digit passcode or Touch ID ([Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/managing-money/budgeting/mint-com)).

## The screens put balances left and budgets front and centre

On the **web**, the tabs (Overview, Transactions, Budgets, Goals, Trends, Ways to Save, plus Investments) ran across the top right. A left sidebar listed account balances grouped by type ([Get Rich Slowly](https://www.getrichslowly.org/getting-started-with-mint/)). Overview was "your home base", with balances, alerts and budget bars ([Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/managing-money/budgeting/get-started-mint); [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/407663/mint-review-financial-budgeting-app.html)). Transactions was "a day-by-day list" in which every row could be edited ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/tag/a-beginners-guide-to-managing-your-money-with-mint/)). Onboarding asked for country and postal code first, then explained the three setup steps alongside familiar bank logos to build trust ([Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/mint-user-onboarding-product-tour)).

The **iOS app before the redesign** (around 2014 to 2019) opened on a scrolling Overview. It showed an Accounts button, cash and credit totals, recent earned and spent, alerts, an "Advice" button that "tends to be sales pitches", and a spending-by-category pie chart. The chart was not accessible to VoiceOver ([AFB AccessWorld](https://afb.org/aw/15/4/15583)). By 2017 credit score and cash-flow modules had been added ([Pratt IXD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2017/01/design-critique-mint-iphone-app/)). The **2021 redesign** pinned **Net Worth and Credit Score in a fixed header**. It added a **"This Month" tab** that put spending, trends and budgets one tap away, and a **swipeable carousel of MintSights cards** that also carried partner offers ([Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/196/all-new-mint-ios-app-launched-to-empower-financial-freedom); [Intuit](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/315/mint-reinvents-app-experience-paving-the-way-to-a-better-money-management-solution)). Accounts were added from a "+" at the top right ([Further help guide](https://learn.hellofurther.com/Individuals/Profile_and_Settings/How_to_Add_Your_Accounts_to_Mint)). To split a transaction on iPhone, you opened a posted transaction, tapped a blue "Split" link at the top right, added line items with categories and amounts, then saved ([Mint community](https://mint.lc.intuit.com/questions/1018176-how-do-i-split-transactions-in-my-iphone-app)).

Several layout details remain **unknown**: the exact iOS tab-bar labels for any version (the oft-recalled "Overview / Transactions / Budgets / Trends / More" is unconfirmed), transaction row anatomy, whether the category picker had two levels, Mint's brand colours and typography, and whether there was a home-screen widget. The old help paths mention an "Updates" screen, so one version may have used that label for home. On mobile, users were dropped straight into the app after their first account connected, "with no tour, no tooltips" ([Appcues](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/mint-user-onboarding-product-tour)).

## Users loved automation and hated offers, sync failures and neglect

Reviewers praised the single screen showing "accounts, bills, and budgets" ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/407663/mint-review-financial-budgeting-app.html)), automatic categorization with colourful charts, custom reminders and the free credit score ([Listen Money Matters](https://www.listenmoneymatters.com/mint-review/)). The shutdown showed what users valued most. Credit Karma kept spending and net-worth tracking, but "setting monthly budgets and customized categories didn't make the cut" ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service)). The outcry centred on losing budgets, categories, bill reminders and goals ([Wallet Hacks](https://wallethacks.com/migrating-from-mint-to-credit-karma-it-was-not-good/)). One user summed up the reaction to the news: "This is the peril of relying on free services" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html)).

The complaints fell into four groups. First, an app "cluttered with partner offers", from TurboTax to credit cards and loans ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/intuit-mint-2022)). Second, glitchy connections and duplicate transactions, for example after card reissues ([MintActivate](https://www.mintactivate.com/bank-account-connection-issues-resolving-duplicate-transactions-in-mint)). Third, too many transactions needing manual recategorizing ([Abacus Wealth](https://abacuswealth.com/five-mint-problems-and-their-solutions/)). Fourth, "slow and missing updates for years" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html)). Reviewers also noted there was no support for joint or shared accounts. For Paul's single-user, ad-free, self-hosted app, the first and fourth problems disappear by design. The second and third remain the real risks.

## Intuit folded Mint into Credit Karma because referrals never paid

Mint was free. It earned money from referral fees on credit cards, loans, insurance and investment accounts, from ads, and from paid credit monitoring ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-mint-make-money/)). Val Agostino, Mint's first product manager and now CEO of Monarch, wrote that the referral model "was never able to cover our data costs." He also said it misaligned incentives, because the offers that paid the highest fees were rarely the best products for users ([Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/blog/mint-shutting-down)). That quote comes from a snippet. Intuit never reported Mint's revenue or profit, and a claimed $50M in 2022 referral revenue is **unsourced and should be disregarded**.

Bloomberg broke the shutdown news on **November 1, 2023** ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-11-01/intuit-winds-down-personal-finance-app-mint-shifts-users-to-credit-karma)). The stated rationale was consolidation into Credit Karma, which Intuit had bought in 2020 and which runs the same lead-generation model at far larger scale. **Dates conflict across sources.** The first cut-off is reported as December 31, 2023 or January 1, 2024, and the final closure as March 23 or March 24, 2024 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Mint)). March 23, 2024 is the most common figure. **The Credit Karma price also conflicts.** An Engadget snippet and Hacker News commenters say $4.7B ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38114512)), while the researcher believed the widely reported figure is about $7.1B ([Wikipedia: Credit Karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Karma)). Neither was verified. RIABiz linked the shutdown to Fidelity cutting off free screen-scraped data weeks earlier ([RIABiz](https://riabiz.com/a/2023/11/16/embattled-mintcom-is-shutting-for-good-weeks-after-fidelity-finally-cut-off-free-screen-scraped-data-that-was-at-the-heart-of-its-business-model)). That is the publication's interpretation, not Intuit's stated reason. Intuit's CEO later said the shutdown went "better than expected" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-10/intuit-s-shutdown-of-mint-has-gone-better-than-thought-ceo-says)). For Canadians it went worse. Mint's Canadian users reportedly had no equivalent migration path, and Monarch launched in Canada to pick them up ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/as-intuit-winds-down-mint-financial-planning-app-monarch-money-comes-to-canada/)). That account comes from a single source and is unverified.

## Monarch and Simplifi come closest; Desjardins sync stays shaky

No app has taken over Mint's free, full-budgeting position. **Monarch Money** (about US$99.99/year) is the most-cited successor. Ex-Mint staff built it, and it offers rollover and "Flex" budgeting ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/finance/learn/best-budget-apps); [pocketclear](https://pocketclear.app/blog/monarch-money-vs-copilot.html)). **Quicken Simplifi** (about US$48/year) "is probably" "the closest experience to what Mint was" ([financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/)). **Copilot** (about US$95/year) sets the bar for iOS polish and has a categorizer that learns from corrections, but in practice it supports only US banks ([dzing](https://www.dzing.money/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money-full-comparison/); [waypointbudget](https://waypointbudget.com/blog/budget-app-bank-sync-canada-2026)). **YNAB** (US$109/year) uses a different, zero-based method. **Rocket Money** is US-only ([unifiedbankings](https://unifiedbankings.com/blog/rocket-money-canada)). Credit Karma is free but has no category budgets. Among self-hosted options, **Actual Budget** syncs through SimpleFIN or GoCardless ([Actual docs](https://actualbudget.org/docs/transactions/importing/)), and Firefly III relies on CSV import. Many 2026 prices come from blogs run by competing vendors and need re-checking.

For Paul in Quebec, the key constraint is that Canada still relies on screen-scraping aggregators. Vendor blogs describe Plaid's Desjardins coverage both as "uneven" and as working for Monarch and YNAB ([mozaicfinance](https://mozaicfinance.com/blog/best-personal-finance-app-quebec-2026/)). This tension is unresolved, and nobody verified a Desjardins success rate. Paul's server already uses Plaid, so his app takes on the same risk. A CSV or OFX import fallback would be insurance against it, but it would require a server change. French-language support in the commercial alternatives was also not confirmed, which gives a bilingual en-CA/fr-CA app a genuine advantage.

## Conclusion

Paul's spec already reproduces the part of Mint that survived as Credit Karma, plus the category budgets that Credit Karma threw away. The remaining Mint "feel" comes mostly from the transaction-hygiene layer: rules, renames, splits, per-transaction hiding and rollover. That layer is what made Mint's numbers trustworthy month after month, and almost all of it needs server storage. The sensible next step is to bundle those items into a single server-change proposal for Paul (rules, payee renames, splits, exclusion flag, tags or notes, rollover), rather than requesting them one at a time. In parallel, the client-only wins can ship now: pace-based bar colours, chart drill-down, an "Everything Else" budget, merchant trends, suggested budgets, CSV export and full fr-CA localization.

Mint's history also suggests where an app like this tends to break. It wasn't missing features. It was unreliable data and features that served the business rather than the user. Paul's app drops the offers by design, but it inherits Mint's dependence on fragile aggregation, and Desjardins-on-Plaid is the least-verified link in the chain. Testing that connection early, and keeping a file-import fallback on the server roadmap, matters more to the app's success than any screen.
