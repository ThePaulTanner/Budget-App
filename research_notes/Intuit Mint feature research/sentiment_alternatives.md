# Intuit Mint: User Sentiment (2006-2024) and Post-Shutdown Alternatives (2025-2026)

> Method note: All direct page fetches (NerdWallet, Hacker News, Monarch blog, TechCrunch, Engadget, Wikipedia, Reddit, robberger.com, waypointbudget.com, mozaicfinance.com) were blocked by the network egress proxy in this session. Findings below come from web-search result snippets and search-engine summaries of those pages, so they have not been read in full context. Many 2026 comparison pages are SEO blogs run by competing budgeting apps (e.g., Tally, PocketClear, Waypoint Budget, Mozaic, Finny, TrackMyStack); treat their claims, especially about their own product, as vendor marketing. Reddit threads could not be accessed directly; Reddit sentiment is reported second-hand (CNBC, Teamblind threads, blog summaries).

## Q1. What did users love about Mint, and what did they miss most after the shutdown?

### Takeaway
Users valued Mint as a free, automatic "all accounts in one place" tool with auto-categorization, simple category budgets, and net-worth tracking. What they missed most after the move to Credit Karma was budgeting by category: monthly category budgets, custom categories and goals. Credit Karma kept accounts and net worth but dropped those features.

### Cited Findings
- History: Aaron Patzer created Mint.com. It started with account aggregation from Yodlee, and Intuit bought it in 2009 for about $170M in cash — [Intuit press release](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1019/intuit-to-acquire-mint-com); [TechCrunch "Mint is Yodlee's YouTube"](https://techcrunch.com/2009/09/18/mint-is-yodlees-youtube/). Mint built the front end and the marketing on top of Yodlee's back end. It paid Yodlee about $2M a year, and Intuit later moved Mint to its own aggregation — [TechCrunch 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/09/18/mint-is-yodlees-youtube/).
- Core value: it tracked bank, credit card, investment and loan balances and transactions in one interface, plus budgets and financial goals — [Wikipedia: Intuit Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Mint) (search snippet).
- Mint had about 3.6M active users (a 2021 figure often cited at the time of the announcement). It shut down on March 23/24, 2024, after an initial cut-off date of Jan 1, 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Mint); [CNBC, Nov 7 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html). Note that sources disagree on the exact final date (Jan 1 vs Mar 23 vs Mar 24, 2024). The likely reason is that the planned Jan 1 cut-off was extended.
- Free plus automatic: Mint was free and supported by targeted offers. It pulled transactions automatically and sorted them into categories — [Tom's Guide review](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/intuit-mint-2022); [Nasdaq review](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/mint-budgeting-app-review).
- What was lost: Credit Karma kept spending and net-worth tracking, but "setting monthly budgets and customized categories didn't make the cut" — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service). Credit Karma lacked the ability to set monthly budgets and track spending and goals by category — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html) (via search summary).
- Credit Karma lacks envelope budgeting, custom categories, monthly per-category targets and savings goals in any meaningful way. The migration moved connected accounts and net worth only — [debtpayofftools / vento / similar blogs via search summary](https://debtpayofftools.com/comparisons/what-happened-to-mint/) (secondary source, lower quality).
- Users said there was no consensus on a free app with all of Mint's budgeting features. One quote: "This is the peril of relying on free services" — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html).
- Intuit's CEO said the shutdown went "better than expected", with more Mint users switching to Credit Karma than anticipated — [Bloomberg, Apr 10 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-10/intuit-s-shutdown-of-mint-has-gone-better-than-thought-ceo-says).

### Inferences
- The "free" price point and the zero-effort aggregation were the hook. Category budgets were what kept people coming back, because they were the feature people protested losing.
- A Mint clone's minimum feature set is: aggregation, auto-categorization with user rules, simple monthly category budgets, custom categories, trends, net worth and goals.

### Gaps
- I could not access App Store reviews or r/mintuit threads directly, so I have no quantified ranking of "most missed" features.
- I found no reliable figure for Mint's user count at its 2010s peak. Commonly repeated numbers (for example "20M+ registered") were not verified in this session.

## Q2. What were Mint's top complaints?

### Takeaway
The main complaints were unreliable connections and sync (broken links, duplicates, re-linking), miscategorization that needed manual fixes, ads and credit card offers, and years of neglect after Intuit bought it (slow updates, features cut). Some data-access breakages, such as Fidelity cutting off screen-scraping, came just before the shutdown.

### Cited Findings
- Common complaints: too many transactions needed manual recategorizing, there were ads and "glitchy integrations", and Intuit served offers ranging from its own products to credit cards and loans — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/intuit-mint-2022); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/mint-budgeting-app-review); [Abacus Wealth "Five Mint problems"](https://abacuswealth.com/five-mint-problems-and-their-solutions/).
- Duplicate transactions were a recurring problem, often linked to account-connection issues. One example is the wave of duplicates after the Target breach card reissues — [egockel Tumblr](https://egockel.tumblr.com/post/75337359197/how-to-fix-dupe-charges-in-mintcom-aka-target); [MintActivate guide](https://www.mintactivate.com/bank-account-connection-issues-resolving-duplicate-transactions-in-mint).
- Neglect: fans were frustrated by "slow and missing updates for years", and this got worse after Intuit bought Credit Karma — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html) (via search summary).
- Hacker News commenters: an early adopter moved their data out after the Intuit acquisition because "it was never going to get better". Others hoped for a QuickBooks integration that never came. One called the shutdown "insanely incompetent", while others pointed out that Intuit paid $4.7B for Credit Karma versus $170M for Mint, so Mint was "a drop in the bucket" — [HN thread 38114512](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38114512) (via search summary).
- Data access: Mint shut down "weeks after Fidelity finally cut off free, screen-scraped data that was at the heart of its business model" — [RIABiz, Nov 2023](https://riabiz.com/a/2023/11/16/embattled-mintcom-is-shutting-for-good-weeks-after-fidelity-finally-cut-off-free-screen-scraped-data-that-was-at-the-heart-of-its-business-model).

### Inferences
- Sync reliability is the biggest risk for any replacement. Screen-scraping fragility hurt Mint, and the same issue still affects Canadian aggregation (see Q6).
- Complaints about ads and offers suggest users accept "free" only if offers stay unobtrusive. Paid apps now market "no ads" as a feature.

### Gaps
- I could not verify complaints about customer support, privacy or slowness from primary sources in this session. They are widely reported anecdotally but were not checked here.
- I found no source cataloguing specific features Intuit removed over time, for example Mint Bills (formerly Check) shutting down.

## Q3. Alternatives: pricing, features vs Mint, migration positioning (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
Most paid Mint replacements now cost about $80 to $110 per year. Monarch is the most-cited full replacement. Simplifi is the cheaper "feels like Mint" choice. Copilot is the design leader but is Apple-first. YNAB is a different, zero-based approach. Rocket Money focuses on subscriptions and bills, and Empower focuses on investments. Credit Karma is free, but its budgeting is gutted.

### Cited Findings
- **Monarch Money**: Core costs $14.99/month or $99.99/year. Plus costs about $199.99/year and was added for power users. There is a 7-day trial — [getfinny Copilot vs Monarch 2026](https://getfinny.app/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money-2026); [NerdWallet best budget apps 2026](https://www.nerdwallet.com/finance/learn/best-budget-apps). It offers rollover budgeting, and "Flex" budgeting collapses categories into three buckets (fixed, variable, savings) — [pocketclear Monarch vs Copilot](https://pocketclear.app/blog/monarch-money-vs-copilot.html). One subscription covers both partners on any device, including Android and web — [dzing comparison](https://www.dzing.money/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money-full-comparison/). Monarch was founded by people from Mint: its CEO was Mint's first product manager — [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/blog/mint-shutting-down). Monarch's co-founder said Nov 1, 2023 "was our biggest day in terms of new users since we launched" — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/02/personal-finance-monarch-intuit-mint/embed/). It has a dedicated "Mint alternative" landing page — [monarch.com/compare/mint-alternative](https://www.monarch.com/compare/mint-alternative). Reviewers cite Forbes calling it "Best Mint Replacement" and WSJ calling it "Best Budgeting App" — [mybudgety](https://mybudgety.com/budgeting/best-mint-alternatives/).
- **Copilot Money**: $13/month or $95/year with a 1-month trial. It has a polished Apple-native design and auto-categorization that learns from corrections, and it supports rollovers. Partners each need their own subscription (about $190/year combined) — [getfinny](https://getfinny.app/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money-2026); [dzing](https://www.dzing.money/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money-full-comparison/). It won an Apple design award and is iOS-focused — [Teamblind thread](https://www.teamblind.com/post/mint-app-users-copilot-is-the-best-budgeting-app-out-around-1zusxoje). Its US App Store build supports only US banks — [waypointbudget](https://waypointbudget.com/blog/budget-app-bank-sync-canada-2026) (vendor blog).
- **YNAB**: $14.99/month or $109/year with a 34-day trial. It uses zero-based budgeting ("give every dollar a job"), and YNAB Together shares one subscription with up to 6 people — [myfinancialfreedomtracker](https://blog.myfinancialfreedomtracker.com/en/budgeting-tool-comparison). Reviewers say its biggest caveat is cost — [financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/).
- **Quicken Simplifi**: about $3.99/month billed annually (~$48/year). Reviewers call it "probably the closest experience to what Mint was", with automatic import, spending categories and budget tracking that work similarly — [financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/). It offered Mint users a 1-year free trial — [Teamblind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/mint-is-shutting-down-any-alternatives-sxdtta6d); [Romit Mehta blog](https://romitmehta.com/2023/12/16/mint-closing-down.html).
- **Rocket Money**: has a free tier. Premium is pay-what-you-want, typically $7 to $14/month. It is mainly a tool for finding and cancelling subscriptions and bills — [myfinancialfreedomtracker](https://blog.myfinancialfreedomtracker.com/en/budgeting-tool-comparison). It is US only: "only available to users located within the United States and with U.S. based banks" — [unifiedbankings](https://unifiedbankings.com/blog/rocket-money-canada); [shelter.money](https://shelter.money/rocket-money-alternative-canada).
- **Empower (formerly Personal Capital)**: free dashboard with an investment-fee analyzer, a retirement planner with Monte Carlo simulation, and net worth tracking. It is monetized by pitching advisory services ($100k minimum, 0.89% AUM) — [myfinancialfreedomtracker](https://blog.myfinancialfreedomtracker.com/en/budgeting-tool-comparison). Users report sync issues and calls from advisors — [Teamblind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/empower-per-vs-monarch-vs-copilot-dg33qmaw).
- **Credit Karma**: the official Intuit destination and free. It has net worth and spending but no category budgets or custom categories — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service).
- **PocketGuard**: has a free plan. Plus costs CA$17.99/month or CA$103.99/year. Its signature feature is "safe-to-spend" — [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/pocketguard/pricing); [financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/). It is described as the closest *automatic* Mint replacement — [financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/).
- **Tiller**: $79/year with a 30-day trial. It syncs bank data into Google Sheets or Excel — [sheetlink](https://sheetlink.app/tiller-money-pricing-2026).
- **Lunch Money**: about $10/month or $100/year and uses Plaid sync — [elektrafi / durhampost via search summary](https://elektrafi.io/blog/best-budgeting-personal-finance-apps-in-canada-for-2026). Pricing details are unverified; Lunch Money has historically used pay-what-you-want pricing.
- Market summary: most Mint alternatives now cost about $95 to $110 per year — [tallyfi](https://tallyfi.app/blog/mint-shut-down-alternatives-2026) (vendor blog).

### Inferences
- No mainstream app took over Mint's *free* plus *full budgeting* position. The free options (Credit Karma, Empower, Rocket Money's free tier) each drop part of the Mint experience.
- Shared-household pricing is a real point of difference: Monarch and YNAB include partners, while Copilot charges per user.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm 2026 prices directly from vendor pricing pages, because fetches were blocked. Lunch Money and Simplifi prices in particular should be re-verified.
- I have no verified detail on each app's categorization-rule engine (rule conditions, retroactive application) or bill-calendar implementation.

## Q4. Which alternative is considered closest to Mint, and which UX ideas could a clone borrow?

### Takeaway
There are two camps. Monarch is the "spiritual successor": built by ex-Mint people, most features, most often named "best Mint replacement". Simplifi "feels most like Mint", with similar workflow and a low price. Copilot is the UX benchmark. A clone could borrow Monarch's rollover and Flex buckets, Copilot's learning categorizer and polish, PocketGuard's "safe-to-spend", Rocket Money's subscription detection, and YNAB's shared plans.

### Cited Findings
- "Monarch Money is the most-cited Mint replacement, founded by ex-Mint engineers explicitly to be what Mint should have become ... closest spiritual successor to Mint's feel" — [financialfitnesspassport / financialaha summary](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/).
- "If you want the closest experience to what Mint was, Simplifi is probably it" — [financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/).
- On forums, users report Monarch connecting all 30+ of their accounts and prefer it to Mint. Simplifi is called "much better than Mint" — [Teamblind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/mint-is-shutting-down-any-alternatives-sxdtta6d).
- Monarch's Flex budgeting sits between strict zero-based budgeting and loose tracking (fixed, variable and savings buckets), and its rollover carries unused funds forward — [pocketclear](https://pocketclear.app/blog/monarch-money-vs-copilot.html).
- Copilot: Apple-native polish and a categorizer that learns from user corrections — [dzing](https://www.dzing.money/blog/copilot-money-vs-monarch-money-full-comparison/).
- PocketGuard: a "safe-to-spend" figure — [financialaha](https://www.financialaha.com/articles/mint-alternatives-after-shutdown/).
- Rocket Money: finds and cancels recurring charges — [myfinancialfreedomtracker](https://blog.myfinancialfreedomtracker.com/en/budgeting-tool-comparison).
- A counter-view exists in "Why I stopped recommending Monarch" — [balancepro](https://www.balancepro.app/blog/why-i-stopped-using-monarch-money). This is a competitor blog, and I did not see its content.

### Inferences
- For a Mint clone, the closest match to what Mint users were used to is Simplifi-style simple category budgets plus Monarch-style optional rollover and Flex modes, with Copilot-level visual polish and learned categorization.

- I could not access Reddit polls or upvote-weighted consensus directly.

## Q5. Self-hosted and open-source replacements and their bank-data approaches

### Takeaway
Actual Budget is the most active self-hosted option. It does envelope budgeting and offers optional bank sync via SimpleFIN (US/Canada) and GoCardless (EU, closed to new sign-ups since July 2025), and also Pluggy.ai. Firefly III is a powerful double-entry ledger that relies on CSV import or separate importer tools. Maybe Finance was archived in mid-2025.

### Cited Findings
- Actual Budget supports bank sync via SimpleFIN, GoCardless or Pluggy.ai — [Actual docs: importing](https://actualbudget.org/docs/transactions/importing/). GoCardless stopped accepting new Bank Account Data accounts from July 2025, though existing users keep working — [Actual docs: GoCardless](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/gocardless/). Actual started as a startup and was open-sourced by its founder — [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/firefly-iii/?platform=self-hosted).
- Firefly III is a self-hosted PHP app that needs a server (NAS, VPS or Pi). It imports via CSV and optional bank-API importers, with no built-in automatic sync — [learnwithhasan](https://learnwithhasan.com/self-hosted-apps/firefly-iii/); [expensesorted](https://www.expensesorted.com/blog/147_firefly_iii).
- Maybe Finance: development stopped in mid-2025, the last release was v0.6.0 in July 2025, and the repository is archived — [dev.to selfhostingsh](https://dev.to/selfhostingsh/actual-budget-vs-maybe-1e5m). The original Maybe app depended on Plaid, Finicity and Auth0 — [alternativeto](https://alternativeto.net/software/firefly-iii/?platform=self-hosted).
- Synci offers managed SimpleFIN sources for UK, EU and NZ banks — [synci.io](https://synci.io/destinations/actual-budget).

### Inferences
- Plaid itself is rarely used by self-hosted apps because of developer-account costs and compliance. SimpleFIN Bridge (a low-cost consumer aggregator based on MX) is the common route in the US and Canada.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether SimpleFIN Bridge reliably supports Desjardins. Any community forks of Maybe (e.g., "Sure") were not confirmed by search.

## Q6. Canadian bank support (Quebec / Desjardins)

### Takeaway
Canada has no live open banking, so every app depends on screen-scraping aggregators (Plaid, Flinks, Yodlee, MX). Desjardins reportedly connects through Plaid to Monarch and YNAB, but the connection is described as uneven. Rocket Money is US only, and Copilot is effectively US only. Canadian-focused options include Wealthica (strong on investments) and Quebec or Canadian apps using Flinks.

### Cited Findings
- Plaid, Yodlee and Flinks underpin most Canadian bank linking, and all of them screen-scrape because Canada hasn't implemented open banking — [waypointbudget](https://waypointbudget.com/blog/budget-app-bank-sync-canada-2026) (vendor blog).
- Monarch and YNAB rely on Plaid, which has "uneven coverage of TD, Desjardins, and many smaller credit unions". Another source says Desjardins connects through Plaid to Wealthsimple, Mozaic, Monarch and YNAB — [richify / mozaicfinance via search summary](https://mozaicfinance.com/blog/best-personal-finance-app-quebec-2026/) (vendor blogs; the two descriptions are partly in tension).
- Monarch is officially available in the US and Canada, but Canadian connectivity is "hit-or-miss". Copilot has limited Canadian connectivity and supports only US banks in the US App Store. Simplifi's Canadian connections are "inconsistent" — [waypointbudget / modestmoney via search summary](https://www.modestmoney.com/monarch-money-vs-simplifi/).
- Monarch costs about CA$22/month, has limited connections to smaller Canadian credit unions, and some users report sync problems — [mybudgety](https://mybudgety.com/budgeting/best-mint-alternatives/).
- Rocket Money does not work in Canada — [unifiedbankings](https://unifiedbankings.com/blog/rocket-money-canada).
- PocketGuard claims Canadian support, but it documents persistent issues with "most major Canadian banks" — [elektrafi / sheetlink via search summary](https://sheetlink.app/pocketguard-pricing-2026).
- Lunch Money (Plaid) reportedly works with most Canadian institutions, though small credit unions may need manual updates — [elektrafi](https://elektrafi.io/blog/best-budgeting-personal-finance-apps-in-canada-for-2026).
- Wealthica has a free basic plan, premium from about $6.25/month, strong investment analytics and a secondary "Budget Wizard" — [mybudgety](https://mybudgety.com/budgeting/best-mint-alternatives/).

### Inferences
- For a Desjardins user, the realistic choice is Monarch or YNAB (Plaid), a Flinks-based Canadian app, or a self-built solution using CSV/OFX import as a fallback. A Mint clone for Quebec should support several aggregators (Plaid plus Flinks) and good file import, plus French localization.

### Gaps
- No first-hand Reddit r/PersonalFinanceCanada data. I have no verified Desjardins success rate on any aggregator, and no confirmation of French-language support in Monarch, YNAB or Copilot.
- I could not verify Credit Karma's Mint-migrated features for Canadians. The migration appears to have been US-only, but this is unconfirmed. I also have no sourced information on Mint's own historical Canadian availability.
- Status of the Canadian consumer-driven banking framework rollout in 2026 was not verified.
