## Founding: Aaron Patzer, the year, the motivation, early funding and the TechCrunch40 win

### Takeaway
Aaron Patzer founded Mint in 2006 because Quicken and Microsoft Money were too much work to use. He raised a $4.7M Series A (April 2007), a $12M Series B (March 2008) and a $14M Series C (August 2009), about $31M in total. He launched the product publicly at TechCrunch40 in September 2007 and won the $50,000 top prize.

### Cited Findings
- Mint.com was founded in 2006 by Aaron Patzer as a personal financial management service for the US and Canada. — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/mint/); [Aaron Patzer, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Patzer)
- Motivation: at about age 25, Patzer was working around 75 hours a week and was frustrated by the extra hours needed to manage his finances in Microsoft Money or Quicken. — [Topgrading case study](https://blog.topgrading.com/case-studies/mint); [7x7 "Hot 20 2010"](https://www.7x7.com/hot-20-2010-aaron-patzer-founder-of-mint-com-1779510305.html); [Big Think, "The Minting of Mint.com"](https://bigthink.com/videos/the-minting-of-mintcom/)
- Patzer reportedly spent about seven months coding largely alone before launch. — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/videos/the-minting-of-mintcom/) / [OwnerPreneur case study](https://ownerpreneur.com/case-studies/mint-from-idea-to-170m-acquisition-aaron-patzers-journey/) (secondary sources; the exact duration is not confirmed against a primary interview)
- **Series A: $4.7M, April 2007**, led by Shasta Ventures, with First Round Capital and individual angels including Ram Shriram (an early Google investor and founding board member). — [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/17608/mint-raises-47-million-in-series-a-funding)
- **Series B: $12M, March 2008**, led by Benchmark Capital. All Series A investors also took part, including Shasta and First Round. — [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/18180/mint-secures-12-million-in-series-b-funding); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/mintcom-raises-12-million-in-series-b-funding-led-by-benchmark-capital); [TechCrunch, "Mint Gets A Mint," 2008-03-05](https://techcrunch.com/2008/03/05/mint-gets-a-mint/)
- **Series C: $14M, August 2009**, led by DAG Ventures, with new investor Founders Fund and existing investors Benchmark, Shasta, First Round and Sherpalo Ventures. — [TechCrunch, "Full Details On Mint's $14 Million Series C Round," 2009-08-12](https://techcrunch.com/2009/08/12/full-details-on-mints-14-million-series-c-round)
- Total venture funding is reported as "over $31 million," from Shasta, First Round, DAG and others. — [Aaron Patzer, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Patzer) (consistent with 4.7 + 12 + 14 = $30.7M plus any seed or angel money)
- **TechCrunch40 (September 2007):** Mint launched publicly at TechCrunch40 and won the Top Company award and its $50,000 prize. — [TechCrunch, 2007-09-18](https://techcrunch.com/2007/09/18/mint-wins-techcrunch40-50000-award/); [Scobleizer, 2007-09-19](https://scobleizerblog.wordpress.com/2007/09/19/techcrunch-40-winner-mint/)
- At launch, Mint pulled in bank, credit union and credit card transactions, categorized them automatically with "patent-pending" technology, and alerted users to upcoming bills, low balances and unusual spending. — [TechCrunch 2007 / search summary](https://techcrunch.com/2007/09/18/mint-wins-techcrunch40-50000-award/)

### Inferences
- The company was founded in 2006 and the product launched in September 2007. Some secondary sources (for example rip.so, which lists "Mint (2007 - 2024)") date Mint from its 2007 launch. A secondary source that says Patzer launched "three years later" conflicts with the 2006 to 2007 timeline and should be ignored.
- The Series C closed only about a month before Intuit's acquisition offer (September 2009). This suggests the round helped set a valuation floor for the deal.

### Gaps
- No seed or angel round amount, and no founding month, was found in a primary source.
- Valuations for each round were not found.

## Growth: users over time, mobile apps and redesigns

### Takeaway
Mint went from about 160K users six months after launch to about 1.5M at acquisition (September 2009), more than 3M in 2010, more than 10M by mid-2012 and more than 20M registered users by about 2016. The iPhone app shipped in December 2008 and the Android app in May 2010. Note that the 20M figure counts registered users; active users later were far lower (see the last section).

### Cited Findings
- About 160,000 users six months after the TechCrunch40 launch (around March 2008). — [TechCrunch, "Mint Gets A Mint," 2008-03-05](https://techcrunch.com/2008/03/05/mint-gets-a-mint/); [AppSamurai](https://appsamurai.com/blog/mobile-app-success-story-how-mint-did-it/)
- **1.5M+ users at acquisition (September 2009).** The site claimed to track nearly $200B in transactions and $50B in assets. — [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/intuit-to-acquire-mintcom-for-170-million); [TechCrunch, 2009-09-14](https://techcrunch.com/2009/09/14/the-value-of-techcrunch50-mint-acquired-by-intuit-for-170m-two-years-after-winning-tc40/)
- **More than 3M users in fiscal 2010.** Intuit said Mint "more than doubled its user base in fiscal 2010." — [Intuit 8-K, FY2010 earnings release (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012310079160/f56699exv99w01.htm) (attribution to this specific filing comes from the search summary)
- **More than 10M users by mid-2012.** — search summary citing [Intuit 10-Q FY2012](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000089687812000048/intu013112-10q.htm) and [TechCrunch, 2012-05-20](https://techcrunch.com/2012/05/20/from-a-tc40-win-to-a-170m-intuit-acquisition-mint-com-tells-all/) (**uncertain:** I could not confirm which document gives the exact figure)
- **More than 20M users by about 2016.** — search summary (sources listed: [Wikipedia, Intuit Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Mint); [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/mint-com-business-model-how-does-mint-com-make-money/)) (**uncertain:** secondary sources only; this is almost certainly cumulative registered users, not active users)
- **iPhone app:** released Monday, December 15, 2008, but it did not appear in the App Store until about 1 AM on Friday, December 19, 2008. It quickly reached the top of the App Store finance charts. — [Finovate](https://finovate.com/chase_bank_mint_top_the_charts_with_new_iphone_apps/)
- **Android app:** Intuit announced a free Mint.com app on the Android Market on May 3, 2010. — [Intuit investor press release](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/956/correcting-and-replacing-android-users-can-manage-money-on-the-go-with-new-mint-com-application)
- **iPad-native app:** released in 2011. — [MacRumors forum thread](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/mint-com-releases-ipad-native-app.1263968/) (forum source; the exact date was not confirmed)
- **2014, Mint Bills:** Intuit agreed on May 27, 2014 to buy Check (formerly PageOnce, a mobile bill-pay rival with 10M registered users) for about $360M. The deal closed June 16, 2014 at about $369M in total consideration, and Check was later renamed Mint Bills. — [Intuit press release](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/618/intuit-to-acquire-check); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/55380/intuit-acquires-mobile-bill-pay-company-check); [Intuit 10-K FY2014](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000089687814000163/fy14q410-kdocument.htm); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/benkepes/2014/05/28/intuit-moves-deeper-into-personal-finance-acquires-mobile-bill-payment-vendor-check/)
- A 10-year retrospective (2017) described Mint as a former first mover that "must work to stay relevant." — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/10-years-first-mover-mint-working-stay-relevant/)

### Inferences
- Growth was fastest from 2009 to 2012, when Mint went from 1.5M to more than 10M users. Coverage after 2016 shows no new user milestones, which fits a product that had stopped growing.

### Gaps

## The Intuit acquisition (2009): price, rationale and changes under Intuit

### Takeaway
Intuit agreed on September 11, 2009 (announced September 14) to buy Mint for about $170M in cash, including assumed stock options, and closed the deal on November 2, 2009. Mint moved into Intuit's "Other Businesses" segment as part of its "Connected Services" strategy. Aggregation then moved from Yodlee to Intuit's own technology during 2010.

### Cited Findings
- Intuit signed a definitive agreement on **September 11, 2009** to acquire Mint Software Inc. "in a cash transaction valued at approximately $170 million, including the assumption of Mint outstanding stock options." — [Intuit 10-K FY2009 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012309043307/f51041e10vk.htm); [Intuit 10-Q (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012309068379/f53802e10vq.htm)
- The deal was publicly reported on September 14, 2009. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2009/09/14/the-value-of-techcrunch50-mint-acquired-by-intuit-for-170m-two-years-after-winning-tc40/); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/intuit-buys-mint-software-for-170m); [Time](https://time.com/archive/6906464/intuit-buys-mint-com-the-future-of-personal-finance/)
- **Closed November 2, 2009.** — [SEC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012309068379/f53802e10vq.htm) (per search summary)
- **Rationale:** Intuit bought Mint "to expand its online personal finance offerings in support of its Connected Services strategy," and placed it in the Other Businesses segment. — [Intuit SEC filings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000896878/000095012309068379/f53802e10vq.htm)
- Patzer was 29 when he sold the company. — [Business Lifestyle Substack](https://businesslifestyle.substack.com/p/the-mintcom-story-he-sold-his-company); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Patzer)
- Commentary at the time asked why Mint sold rather than staying independent. Mint's dependence on Yodlee was a theme, for example the Hacker News thread "Mint Is Yodlee's YouTube." — [rc3.org](http://rc3.org/2009/09/19/why-did-mint-com-sell-to-intuit/); [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=830075)
- **Aggregation move from Yodlee to Intuit:** from January 2010, all new Mint users were served by Intuit's aggregation rather than Yodlee. Existing users were migrated over the following months, and by December 2010 Yodlee no longer powered Mint. During the transition, users reported accounts that stopped updating and missing debit transactions. — [CNN Money, 2010-12-02, "Yodlee no longer powers Mint.com's data aggregation tools"](https://money.cnn.com/2010/12/02/pf/mint_leaves_yodlee/index.htm)
- Yodlee said publicly that losing Mint did not hurt it badly ("We weren't screwed by Mint's acquisition"). — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/finance-startup-yodlee-we-werent-screwed-by-mints-acquisition)
- Intuit later pulled Mint-style insights into its Quicken desktop product ("Mintified"), before it sold Quicken in 2016. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=231752)
- **Late-life aggregation and Fidelity:** RIABiz reported that Mint relied on screen-scraped data, and that Fidelity Investments had recently cut off free screen-scraping, sending aggregators to a paid, API-based data provider, weeks before the shutdown was announced. RIABiz framed this as a factor in the shutdown. — [RIABiz, 2023-11-16](https://riabiz.com/a/2023/11/16/embattled-mintcom-is-shutting-for-good-weeks-after-fidelity-finally-cut-off-free-screen-scraped-data-that-was-at-the-heart-of-its-business-model) (**this is RIABiz's interpretation; Intuit did not cite Fidelity as a reason**)

### Inferences
- $170M against about 1.5M users works out to roughly $113 per user. Against about $31M raised, the sale returned roughly 5 to 6 times the capital invested (before preferences and dilution).
- Moving off Yodlee cut a third-party cost and dependence, but it caused years of connection-reliability complaints. These became a lasting part of Mint's reputation.

### Gaps
- **Finicity:** I found no source confirming when, or whether, Mint moved to Finicity, Plaid or other aggregators in its later years. The only Finicity hit was generic (Mastercard bought Finicity for about $825M). This remains unverified.
- How the purchase price was allocated (goodwill versus intangibles) could not be pulled because SEC.gov was blocked.

## Business model: lead generation, ads and credit scores. Was Mint profitable?

### Takeaway
Mint was free. It made most of its money from referral (lead-generation) fees on personalized credit card, bank, loan, insurance and investment offers ("Ways to Save"), plus ads and a paid credit-monitoring upsell. A free credit score arrived in late 2014 (Equifax-based) and switched to TransUnion VantageScore 3.0 on October 2, 2017. That is earlier than the "about 2016" in the brief. Mint's first product manager says referral revenue never covered the cost of data. Intuit never broke out Mint's profit and loss.

### Cited Findings
- Revenue came mainly from referral fees on targeted financial products (credit cards, loans, insurance, investment accounts), paid when a user signed up or was approved, plus in-app ads and a premium credit-monitoring service. — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-mint-make-money/); [ProductMint](https://productmint.com/mint-com-business-model-how-does-mint-com-make-money/); [Investopedia (cached)](https://www.investopedia.com.cach3.com/articles/personal-finance/121714/mintcom-top-free-moneytracking-tools.asp.html)
- **On profitability (insider view):** Val Agostino, Mint's first product manager and later CEO of Monarch, wrote that the referral model "was never able to cover our data costs of delivering the service." He also said it misaligned incentives, because the offers paying the highest referral fees were usually not the best products for users. — [Monarch blog, "Mint is shutting down…"](https://www.monarch.com/blog/mint-shutting-down) (quote taken from a search snippet; check wording against the page before quoting)
- One secondary source claims Mint made about $50M in referral fees in 2022, and estimates ARPU at $10 to $15 a year. — [DeleteMyInfo](https://deletemyinfo.com/how-much-does-mint-com-earn-per-user-annually/) / [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-mint-make-money/) (**highly uncertain:** I found no primary source, and Intuit did not report Mint revenue separately)
- Some later explainers say Mint's ad and referral pipeline was hurt by Apple's App Tracking Transparency and Google's privacy changes, and that Mint had become unprofitable "by Intuit's own admission." — search summary citing [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/why-is-the-mint-app-shutting-down/) and similar blogs (**speculative and unverified; I found no Intuit statement admitting Mint was unprofitable**)
- **Free credit score:** launched in late 2014, based on Equifax. From **October 2, 2017**, Mint switched to TransUnion data and the VantageScore 3.0 model. — [Doctor of Credit](https://www.doctorofcredit.com/mint-com-review-free-credit-score-report-monitoring/); [myFICO forum, "Mint is now giving free Equifax Score"](https://ficoforums.myfico.com/t5/General-Credit-Topics/Mint-is-now-giving-free-Equifax-Score/td-p/3384517); [Mint "How Mint works: Credit"](https://mint.intuit.com/how-mint-works/credit)
- A pre-launch 2007 Mint pitch deck exists (for detail on the original monetization plan). — [StartupFundraising teardown](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/mint-com-pre-launch-pitch-deck-teardown)

### Inferences
- The user's "about 2016, TransUnion/Equifax?" is partly right. The sequence was Equifax first (late 2014), then TransUnion VantageScore 3.0 (October 2017).
- Credit Karma, bought by Intuit in 2020, runs the same lead-generation model at much larger scale. That made Mint redundant inside Intuit, which is the most likely business logic behind folding it in.

### Gaps
- Intuit never disclosed Mint's revenue, costs or profitability. "Was Mint profitable?" cannot be answered from primary data. The best evidence is Agostino's insider claim that referral revenue did not cover data costs.
- No sourced launch date was found for the "Ways to Save" branding itself.

## Shutdown: announcement, final date, Intuit's reasons, migration to Credit Karma and user reaction

### Takeaway
Intuit announced on about November 1-2, 2023 that Mint would close and its users would move to Credit Karma. The original closing date was given as January 1, 2024 (some reports say December 31, 2023). It was later moved to **March 23, 2024**. Credit Karma kept net worth tracking, account balances, about three years of transactions, and spending and cash-flow views by category. It dropped monthly and category budgets, custom categories, bill reminders and goals. Users reacted with widespread frustration and moved to Monarch, Simplifi, YNAB and Rocket Money.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg reported on **November 1, 2023** that Intuit was closing Mint and shifting users to Credit Karma. — [Bloomberg, 2023-11-01](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-11-01/intuit-winds-down-personal-finance-app-mint-shifts-users-to-credit-karma); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/mergers-and-acquisitions/intuit-winds-down-personal-finance-app-mint-it-acquired-in-2009)
- The closing date was first given as December 31, 2023 (some sources say Mint would be "integrated with Credit Karma in January 2024") and was later moved to **March 23, 2024**. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html); [Wikipedia, Intuit Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Mint); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service)
- **Intuit's stated framing:** "Mint will go away, but some of the most popular Mint-like features are available on Intuit Credit Karma." Users could bring "the majority of your Mint financial account balances, historical net worth, and 3 years of transactions." Credit Karma would let them track net worth and monitor spending, transactions and cash flow, month over month and by category. — search summary of Intuit's Mint transition FAQ and [TurboTax community thread](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/other-financial-discussions/discussion/what-are-you-moving-to-now-that-mint-is-shutting-down/00/3106588); [20SomethingFinance](https://20somethingfinance.com/mint-shutdown-credit-karma-transition/)
- Intuit explicitly said: "the new experience in Credit Karma does not offer the ability to set monthly and category budgets." — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html)
- Migration worked by logging into Credit Karma from the Mint app, after which the Mint profile was no longer available. Users could also download or delete their Mint data instead. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html)
- Kept in Credit Karma: connected accounts, balances, net worth history, transactions, and spending and cash-flow by category. Not kept: monthly and category budgets, custom categories, bill reminders, goal tracking. — [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/mint-budgeting-app-is-going-away-here-are-some-alternatives/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/mint-app-closing-what-it-means-how-to-pick-a-new-budget-service); [Wallet Hacks, "I Was Shocked After Migrating from Mint to Credit Karma"](https://wallethacks.com/migrating-from-mint-to-credit-karma-it-was-not-good/)
- I found no evidence that Credit Karma later added Mint-style category budgets (as of the sources searched). — [Rocket Money](https://www.rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/mint-app-shutting-down); [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/mint-budgeting-app-is-going-away-here-are-some-alternatives/) (absence of evidence only)
- **Intuit's reasons:** consolidation of its personal finance products under Credit Karma, which Intuit had bought in 2020. — [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/why-is-the-mint-app-shutting-down/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-11-01/intuit-winds-down-personal-finance-app-mint-shifts-users-to-credit-karma)
- **Credit Karma purchase price conflict:** Engadget's text, as shown in a search snippet, says Intuit "spent $4.7 billion" on Credit Karma in late 2020. The widely reported figure is about $7.1B in cash and stock (announced February 2020, closed December 2020). — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html); [Wikipedia, Credit Karma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Karma) (**conflict:** I could not fetch either page to confirm. Verify before using either number. The $7.1B figure comes from my background knowledge and should be checked against Intuit's press release.)
- **User reaction:** CNBC headlined loyal users' disappointment with the quote "This is the peril of relying on free services." The Mint subreddit was in uproar. Users complained that budgets had nowhere to go in Credit Karma, category structures did not transfer, and bill reminders disappeared. — [CNBC, 2023-11-07](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html); [Michael Tsai blog](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2023/11/08/mint-com-replaced-by-credit-karma/); [Wallet Hacks](https://wallethacks.com/migrating-from-mint-to-credit-karma-it-was-not-good/)
- **Competitors benefited:** Monarch Money targeted Mint users, including launching in Canada because Mint's Canadian users were left without a migration path (Credit Karma Canada did not offer the same transfer). Simplifi (Quicken), YNAB and Rocket Money also courted Mint users. — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/as-intuit-winds-down-mint-financial-planning-app-monarch-money-comes-to-canada/); [Monarch blog](https://www.monarch.com/blog/mint-shutting-down); [Rocket Money](https://www.rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/mint-app-shutting-down)

### Inferences
- The exact announcement day is November 1 or 2, 2023, depending on time zone and whether you count the user email or the Bloomberg report. "Early November 2023" is safe.
- Inside Intuit, Mint's referral model was a smaller copy of Credit Karma's. Credit Karma also had about 100M+ members (not verified here). That makes shutting Mint down a cost-saving consolidation more than a reaction to a single event like the Fidelity cutoff.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve Intuit's own press release or the user email text, so the verbatim official reasons are unconfirmed.
- The Canadian transition specifics (whether Canadian users could migrate at all) rest only on BetaKit's framing. Verify before using.
- I found no figures for how many Mint users actually moved to Credit Karma.

## User counts at shutdown

### Takeaway
The only widely cited figure is **3.6 million monthly active users in 2021**, which Bloomberg attributed to Intuit data and which other outlets repeated. That compares with more than 20M registered users claimed around 2016. No 2023 figure was found.

### Cited Findings
- Mint had 3.6M monthly active users in 2021, per Intuit data first reported by Bloomberg and repeated by Engadget, Bloomberg Law, American Banker, BetaKit and LogRocket. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/mergers-and-acquisitions/intuit-winds-down-personal-finance-app-mint-it-acquired-in-2009); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/intuit-is-closing-down-mint-its-popular-free-budget-tracking-app-054145229.html); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/opinion/mint-a-surprising-lesson-in-security); [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/as-intuit-winds-down-mint-financial-planning-app-monarch-money-comes-to-canada/)

### Inferences
- 3.6M MAU against more than 20M registered users means only about one in six registered users was active by 2021. Mint's footprint was much smaller than its headline numbers implied.

### Gaps
- No user count at the time of shutdown (late 2023 or March 2024) was found. The 3.6M figure is from 2021 and should be labeled that way.
- I could not tell whether 3.6M counted web and mobile combined, or US-only users.
